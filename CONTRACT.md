# CONTRACT.md — Pencil

Pencil's product guarantees. Every clause has a stable ID. `rer verify` reports runtime coverage and explicit deferrals under the first-release profile; `pnpm verify:contract:full` retains every TODO obligation. Security, privacy, and operational guarantees are in `BASELINE.md`; a clause here may supersede one of them by naming its `BASE-` ID (WS-003 supersedes `BASE-SECRET-002` for optional externals; BRAND-001 supersedes `BASE-INPUT-003` for public business logos). Capacity and rate limits are in `seed.json → limits`. Times are p95 at those limits. Clauses marked *Policy* have a default that buyers may replace through the named extension point.

## Run and first setup

Use the rer installation route in README.md, then `rer dev`; it prepares a local owner automatically. `rer setup --owner-email EMAIL --owner-name NAME` is optional when choosing local owner details. Open the private one-click RER_OPEN link. A fresh owner finishes the business name and IANA time zone at `/setup`; other app routes send an unfinished owner there. Finished setup enters the app, and established workspaces retain their completed state. Setup preserves existing owner, data and exact private settings, then exits with no app running. Native authentication and session policy follow BASELINE.md. Use `rer describe` for verification, reset, data movement and owner deployment commands; OPERATIONS.md records their effects.

## Scope

Pencil is the owned form and survey platform a US business runs instead of Typeform, SurveyMonkey, Jotform or Google Forms, and against Tally, Fillout and Formbricks. It covers the whole job those products sell: a branching builder with every common question type, calculations, quizzes, hidden fields and prefill, file uploads, signatures and provider-hosted payments; every distribution channel (link, embed, popup, email, SMS, QR, in-product and email-embedded questions, recurring programs); valid collection with partial capture and save-and-resume; submission workflows and approvals; reports with crosstabs, trends and shared views; the integrations the category runs on (Google Sheets, Excel, HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack, Teams, Mailchimp, Notion, Airtable, Zapier, webhooks, an API and MCP); migration from each incumbent; roles, SSO and regulated-buyer controls; and analysis that turns responses into owned, measured actions. Agents go further: they design unbiased studies, read open text for themes and contradictions, propose the follow-up that settles them, and carry an approved finding into an owner's work and check whether it moved the metric.

The first release shipped the feedback-to-action core: operators build short adaptive flows, invite a known audience or publish a public link or embed, review completed responses, use AI to surface evidence-backed findings, and turn one recommendation into an owned action that can be measured and closed. Those active clauses remain the foundation; the target clauses below extend them to the complete category job.

Sibling products keep their own markets, as incumbents do: Pencil links to them and never rebuilds them. Orbit is the CRM, Mango the email-marketing platform, HappyToHelp the help desk, Penny the public feedback board and roadmap, Inkwell the multi-party e-signature service, Dial appointment booking, Crank general workflow automation, Cradle the relational database, Chest file storage, Nook team chat, Vector the issue tracker, Buoy customer-success health, Stall the storefront and Nectar subscription billing. Pencil's own form fields for signatures, payments, uploads and scheduling hand off to those products or to named third parties where the job becomes theirs.

## Clause forms and status

Every clause has a stable ID and is one of two forms.

- An **active clause** is written `**ID — Title.**`. It is declared by a `seed.json` capability, carries tagged runtime or browser tests, and `rer verify` (`pnpm verify:contract`) refuses a release unless every one of them passes. Every active clause in this document is **done**.
- A **target clause** is written `**ID** — **Title.**` and ends with its status: **partial** (some behaviour ships; the cited file or test shows what) or **missing** (no code yet). Each states the behaviour a buyer gets and its *Acceptance* evidence: the journey or test through a public door (route, page, API, command or transport) that proves it. A build round activates a target clause by implementing it, tagging those tests with `contract('ID', …)`, declaring it in a `seed.json` capability and rewriting it in the active form. Where a target clause names an active clause it amends or replaces, that active clause is rewritten in the same change.

The status table at the end lists every clause with its status and evidence path. ACCEPTANCE.md lists every clause's acceptance evidence in the same order.

## Product site

- **SITE-001 — Product page.** The run site's `/` (run-pencil.com, written by `rer build --site`) renders Pencil's own product page for people evaluating the software: what Pencil does, its Run Pencil action to `/run`, and links to the source repository and the documentation. It sets no cookies, loads nothing from third-party origins, links no installed-instance route, and makes claims only about active clauses of this contract: no deferred ANLY, ACT or NOTIFY features, invented metrics, customers, testimonials or pricing. The installed `/` is governed by HOME-001.
- **SITE-002 — Live answer path.** The product page's first screen demonstrates an answer path: choosing an answer on the illustrated card shows the follow-up question that answer leads to, works by keyboard, announces the change to assistive technology, and respects reduced motion. Without JavaScript the card shows one complete question, answer and follow-up.
- **DOCS-001 — Documentation.** `/docs` and `/docs/<slug>` render the product documentation from Markdown in this repository at build time, with navigation, an on-page table of contents and previous/next links. Unknown slugs return 404. Docs pages set no cookies and load nothing from third-party origins.

## Application shell

- **SHELL-001 — Operator shell.** `/app` and every route below it require a valid operator session before the operator document or API data is served. A valid session receives labelled, keyboard-operable navigation to the implemented operator surfaces; the public respondent experience is never an operator navigation destination. `/signin` remains sessionless and is the only entry point to the operator session flow, so an unauthenticated request never receives the operator shell as a fallback.

## Operators

- **OPER-001 — Equal operators.** Every operator can see and act on every flow, audience, response, analysis, and action. There are no roles.
- **OPER-002 — Management.** Operators are invited and removed in settings. Any operator may invite another and may remove any other operator, except that the setup operator can be removed only by themselves and the last operator cannot be removed. There are no roles (OPER-001), so there is no seam that narrows who may do this.
- **OPER-003 — Sign-in depends on mail.** `BASE-ACCESS-001` magic links are delivered through `mail.sender.v1`, so mail is a required external, not an optional one: startup fails under WS-003 naming the missing variable when `MAIL_PROVIDER`, `MAIL_API_KEY`, or `MAIL_FROM` is absent. No deployment starts in a state where no operator can sign in.

### Target: roles, sharing and sign-in

- **ROLE-001** — **Workspace roles.** Each operator holds one role: Owner, Admin, Editor, Analyst or Viewer. Owners and Admins manage operators, installation settings, connections and retention; Editors build, publish and distribute the forms they can access; Analysts read results, run analysis and create actions but cannot edit or publish; Viewers read reports only. Every API route and page checks the role on the server; a refused request names the missing permission and writes nothing. On activation this replaces OPER-001 and the no-roles sentence of OPER-002; the last Owner cannot be removed or demoted. *Acceptance:* a Worker suite signs in as each role and calls every operator route family (flows, publish, audience, responses, export, analysis, actions, settings, connections), asserting allowed and refused outcomes from one role matrix; a browser journey shows an Analyst the results but no Publish control. *Status: missing.*
- **ROLE-002** — **Per-form access and folders.** Forms live in folders; an Admin can restrict a folder or form to named operators, and an operator sees, searches, exports and receives notifications only for forms they can access. Access to respondent identity (identified participants, typed email and phone answers, uploads, signatures, payment details) is a separate grant; without it the ledger, exports and integrations show those values masked. *Acceptance:* a Worker test restricts a form to one Editor and proves a second Editor gets 404 from its page, results, export and API, and an operator without the identity grant receives masked values in the ledger and CSV. *Status: missing.*
- **ROLE-003** — **Single sign-on and provisioning.** Owners can require SAML 2.0 or OpenID Connect sign-in through the business identity provider (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, JumpCloud or the sibling Auth), map IdP groups to ROLE-001 roles, and accept SCIM 2.0 provisioning so a deprovisioned user loses every session within one minute. Magic-link sign-in remains the break-glass path for Owners only when SSO is enforced. *Acceptance:* a Worker test signs in through a test OIDC provider and a signed SAML assertion, maps a group to Analyst, then a SCIM `active:false` PATCH revokes the session and the next request is refused. *Status: missing.*
- **ROLE-004** — **Builder collaboration.** Operators with edit access can leave comments on a draft step, mention another operator (who receives an in-app notification), resolve the thread, and see who last saved each step. Concurrent edits stay safe under FLOW-004's version check. *Acceptance:* a browser journey has two operators comment and resolve on one step; a stale save by the second is refused with the latest version shown. *Status: missing.*

## Workspace

- **WS-001 — Workspace time zone.** Settings hold one IANA time zone for the workspace. First-run setup offers the browser's IANA time zone as an editable default; existing configured workspaces retain their saved zone. Every deadline, sending window, digest send time, and operator-facing date and time is evaluated and displayed in it. A revision records the zone in force when its deadline was set, so changing the workspace zone later cannot move a deadline on an open revision.
- **WS-002 — Sending hours.** Settings hold a daily sending window in the WS-001 zone, defaulting to 09:00–17:00 every day. Scheduled mail — reminders and digests — that falls due outside the window is held and sent when the window next opens; nothing is dropped for falling outside it. Mail an operator triggers directly, and every magic link, is sent immediately regardless of the window.
- **WS-003 — Optional externals are configuration, not degradation.** *Supersedes `BASE-SECRET-002`.* Pencil starts only when every variable marked required in `seed.json → env` is present, and fails at startup naming the first missing one. `ai.provider.v1` and `task.destination.v1` are declared optional: with either absent the deployment starts and serves, every clause that does not name that adapter holds in full, and each feature offered by the implemented profile that does name it is shown as unavailable alongside page-local connection setup, with deployment variables retained as an alternative. Features deferred by the declared contract profile are omitted from the interface until implemented. No feature silently produces a reduced result instead.

## Business branding

- **BRAND-001 — Saved business identity.** *Supersedes `BASE-INPUT-003` for business logos only.* Operators save the business name, one supporting line, an accent in #rrggbb format, and an optional PNG, JPEG or WebP logo within `seed.json → limits.brand`. A fresh workspace asks its owner for the business name at `/setup` and uses neutral defaults until saved. The same saved brand appears in preview, direct and embedded forms, completion and closed states, the selected public landing, and new-response email. Email includes the saved name and supporting line as literal text and escaped HTML, the saved accent, and any validated logo as inline CID bytes with no remote resources; removing the logo affects the next dispatch run. The accent's button text has at least 4.5:1 contrast; a readable foreground is selected for other accent text. Logo removal takes effect everywhere. Authenticated server uploads check size, declared MIME and content signatures, refusing SVG. Logos are served from this installation with the validated Content-Type and nosniff, with no third-party request. This clause supersedes BASE-INPUT-003 for business logos only: their bytes live in D1 and are deliberately public inline images, included in deployment exports, rather than private R2 downloads. Business identity is independent of Pencil's operator authentication branding.

## Flows

- **FLOW-001 — Lifecycle.** A new flow is draft; publishing makes it open; an open flow can close; a closed flow can reopen the same published revision only after no deadline or target condition remains satisfied, or it can archive; a draft can archive; and an archived flow cannot reopen. Draft public URLs return 404, closed revisions show the standard closed page, and archived public URLs return 404. Archiving removes the flow from default operator views without deleting its revisions, responses, analyses, or actions.
- **FLOW-002 — Ordered steps.** A draft contains an ordered list of steps: at most one welcome screen, which is optional and can only be first; one or more questions; and one or more named completion screens. Operators can add, edit, duplicate, reorder, and delete them.
- **FLOW-003 — Question types.** A question is short text, long text, email, single choice, multiple choice, rating, priority, dropdown, yes/no, NPS, number, date, or consent; it has a prompt, optional why-we-ask text, required flag, and applicable editable type-specific options within the limits in `seed.json`. The builder canvas uses the same respondent controls.
- **FLOW-004 — Saved edits.** A field-valid builder write includes the draft version it read and may leave the graph incomplete until publication. One Save per step acknowledges a single version-checked transaction containing its content, choices and answer paths. Invalid writes change none of those fields. A current-version edit is persisted before the builder shows it as saved; a stale edit is rejected with the latest version and cannot overwrite another operator's edit. Leaving or reloading after the saved indicator cannot lose the accepted edit.
- **FLOW-005 — Branching.** A single-choice or dropdown answer can match equality against a stable choice ID; yes/no and consent answers can match boolean equality; rating, priority, NPS, number and calendar-date answers can match equality or ordered comparison; and a multiple-choice answer can match any or all selected stable choice IDs. Priority equality compares the complete ordering and ordered comparisons use a choice's rank. Preview shows the ordered rule evaluations and chosen next step used by the published runtime, using the same predicates. *Policy: `flow.branch-selection.v1`; default: first matching rule in displayed order, then the required default path.*
- **FLOW-006 — Publish validation.** Publishing runs every FLOW-012 through FLOW-014 validation and is rejected with all offending stable step IDs and reasons; rejection creates no revision and leaves the draft editable.
- **FLOW-007 — Preview isolation.** Preview runs the current draft with test answers, identifies itself as preview, sends no invitations or external effects, and never creates a response or changes result counts.
- **FLOW-008 — Published revisions.** Publishing creates an immutable numbered revision and makes it the current revision resolved by the flow's canonical direct and embed URL. Every invitation, first-interaction session permitted by RESP-013, completed response, analysis, and export records the revision it used; merely loading the page creates no record.
- **FLOW-009 — Live edits.** Editing an open flow changes a new draft only. Existing invitations and first-interaction sessions stay pinned to their recorded revision; publishing the draft affects only later direct or embedded sessions and later invitation launches.
- **FLOW-010 — Closure.** An operator may close a flow immediately or configure a revision-specific deadline or completed-response target. A deadline is an instant in the WS-001 zone recorded with that revision. A manual flow close closes every revision's admission; a deadline or target closes only that revision's admission atomically.
- **FLOW-011 — Duplication.** Duplicating a flow creates an unlinked draft containing its latest draft or published structure and settings, but no audience members, invitations, responses, analyses, actions, delivery history, or external IDs.
- **FLOW-012 — Valid references.** Every branch predicate references an existing structured question and stable choice when applicable, and every branch destination references an existing question or completion screen.
- **FLOW-013 — Reachable termination.** The flow's first step is its welcome screen or its first question, every step is reachable from it on at least one path, and every possible path reaches one completion screen without a cycle.
- **FLOW-014 — Valid questions.** Every question has a non-empty prompt within the text limit, every structured question has valid distinct options within its type limits, and the flow contains at least one question.
- **FLOW-015 — Stable structure IDs.** A step and each of its choices receive stable IDs when created; editing, reordering, publishing, exporting, and importing preserve them, while duplication creates new IDs. Operators enter choice labels; answer values are generated from them, remain unchanged when a saved label is renamed, and can be edited under Advanced.
- **FLOW-016 — Published policy snapshot.** A published revision stores the evaluated configuration for `flow.branch-selection.v1`, `response.identity.v1`, and `flow.response-admission.v1`; later policy changes affect only a newly published revision.
- **FLOW-017 — Revision admission.** A new direct or embedded visit starts the current revision; an invitation starts its recorded revision; and a returning first-interaction session resumes its recorded revision. A superseded revision cannot start a new anonymous session and remains reachable only through an existing invitation or session until its admission closes.
- **FLOW-018 — Revision counts.** Deadline and target admission checks use completed responses for that revision only. Reaching either condition cannot close another revision, while manual close and archive close all revisions.
- **FLOW-019 — Answer values by type.** A stored answer is exactly one of: for short text and long text, a string within that type's character limit in `seed.json → limits`; for email, a syntactically valid address stored as the respondent typed it and compared case-insensitively; for single choice and dropdown, one stable choice ID belonging to that question; for multiple choice, a set of distinct stable choice IDs belonging to that question, within its configured minimum and maximum selections; for rating, an integer within the question's configured scale; for priority, a complete ordering of that question's stable choice IDs with no repetition and no omission; for yes/no, a boolean; for NPS, an integer from 0 through 10; for number, a finite number within optional minimum, maximum and decimal precision; for date, a true calendar YYYY-MM-DD value within optional earliest/latest bounds without timezone conversion; for consent, `{accepted: boolean, statement: string}` retaining the immutable published statement, with required consent accepting only true. Nothing else is a valid answer.
- **FLOW-020 — Required applies to the path taken.** A required question blocks progress only on a path that reaches it. A question that the respondent's answers route around is never asked, never recorded as unanswered or missing, and never blocks final submission; publish validation does not require a required question to appear on every path.
- **FLOW-021 — Deleting a question keeps its answers.** Deleting a step in a draft changes only that draft. Answers already collected keep their stable step ID and the revision that asked them, stay in the ledger, results, exports, and existing analyses, and are never rewritten or removed by a later publication. A question first added in a later revision is never reported as unanswered by responses to an earlier revision that did not contain it.

### Target: the complete builder

- **FIELD-001** — **Hidden fields and URL parameters.** An operator declares named hidden fields (for example `utm_source`, `plan`, `order_id`) with a type and whether each is identifying. Values arrive from the link's query string, the embed's data attributes, the in-product SDK (DIST-015) or an invitation, are validated against the declared type, are stored with the response, appear in the ledger, filters, crosstabs, exports and integrations, and can drive branching and recall. Undeclared parameters are discarded. An identifying hidden field makes the response identified and RESP-005's disclosure says so before the first answer; an anonymous flow refuses identifying parameters. *Acceptance:* a browser journey opens a link with declared and undeclared parameters, branches on one, and the CSV export carries only the declared values; a Worker test proves an identifying parameter on an anonymous revision is refused at publish. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-002** — **Matrix, Likert and scale grids.** A matrix question asks several rows on one shared scale (single- or multi-select per row, or a 5- or 7-point Likert), each row with a stable ID; results show a per-row distribution with RSLT-003 denominators, and branching can test any row. *Acceptance:* a Worker test publishes a 4×5 matrix, collects responses, and asserts per-row counts and an export column per row keyed by stable row ID. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-003** — **CSAT, CES and visual rating questions.** CSAT (1–5 satisfaction), CES (1–7 effort) and emoji or thumbs ratings are typed questions with fixed semantics, so programs (PROG-001) compute standard scores: CSAT as the share of 4–5 answers, CES as the mean, both with stated denominators. *Acceptance:* a Worker test collects each type and asserts the computed CSAT percentage and CES mean against hand-computed values. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-004** — **US contact fields.** Phone (stored E.164, displayed in US format, extension allowed), postal address (street, unit, city, two-letter state, five- or nine-digit ZIP, with non-US country as an option), full name split into parts, URL and currency amount in USD are typed questions with validation and formatted export columns. No third-party autocomplete script is loaded on the respondent page (BASE-PUBLIC-001). *Acceptance:* a browser journey enters valid and invalid US phone, ZIP and state values and sees field-specific errors; the export carries normalized columns. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-005** — **Media in questions and choices.** Steps may carry an image with required alt text or a video from an approved host (YouTube or Vimeo via a privacy-enhanced embed only after the respondent clicks to play), and choices may be images with labels. *Acceptance:* a browser journey builds an image-choice question, publish is refused while alt text is empty, and the respondent page makes no third-party request until play is pressed. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-006** — **Randomized order.** An operator may randomize the order of a question's choices (keeping "Other" and "None" anchored) or of a block of questions; the order each respondent saw is stored with the response and exported, so order effects can be analysed. *Acceptance:* a Worker test collects 200 responses and asserts every permutation stored is valid and the presented order is in the export. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-007** — **Scheduling handoff.** A scheduling step hands the respondent to the business's booking page in the sibling Dial, Calendly or Google Calendar appointment schedules, passing name and email only when the respondent typed them and the disclosure names the destination; the booked time returned by the provider's webhook is stored with the response. Pencil does not run availability or calendars itself. *Acceptance:* a Worker test with a Dial/Calendly receiver fixture completes a booking handoff and stores the confirmed time on the response; an anonymous flow passes nothing. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-008** — **Classic page layout.** Besides one question at a time (RESP-001), a flow may use pages that show several questions together with conditional show/hide inside the page, the layout Jotform and Google Forms buyers use. Branching, required-on-path (FLOW-020), progress (RESP-002) and validation apply per page. On activation RESP-001 is amended to name both layouts. *Acceptance:* a browser journey builds a three-page classic form with in-page conditional questions, and a hidden required question never blocks submission. *Status: missing.*
- **FIELD-009** — **Video and audio answers.** A question may accept a recorded video or audio answer (up to an operator-set length) captured in the browser with explicit camera or microphone permission, stored like an upload (FILE-001), transcribed by `ai.provider.v1` when configured, and analysed through its transcript with the clip available to authorized operators. *Acceptance:* a browser journey with a fake media device records a 10-second answer, the operator plays it back through a signed URL, and the transcript appears in the ledger and analysis evidence. *Status: missing.*
- **LOGIC-001** — **Answer recall.** A prompt, choice label, completion screen or email may insert an earlier answer, hidden field or calculated value (`{{name}}`, `{{plan}}`); recall shows only values on the respondent's current path and renders them as escaped text. *Acceptance:* a browser journey answers a name question and sees it in the next prompt; going back and changing it updates the later prompt; an injected `<script>` answer renders inert. *Status: missing.*
- **LOGIC-002** — **Known-data prefill.** An invitation, identified in-product survey or CRM-linked link may prefill questions from the contact's stored fields (AUD-001) or a connected CRM record (INTEG-003 to INTEG-005); prefilled answers are visibly marked, editable by the respondent unless locked by the operator, and recorded as prefilled in the response. Anonymous collection never prefills. *Acceptance:* a Worker test launches invitations to contacts with a company field, the respondent sees it prefilled, edits it, and the ledger shows the edited value with a prefill marker. *Status: missing.*
- **LOGIC-003** — **Variables and calculations.** Operators define numeric and text variables computed from answers with arithmetic, conditionals, rounding and date differences (for example a price total, a lead score or a BMI); calculations are typed, validated at publish for missing references and division by zero, usable in branching, recall, payments (PAY-001) and results, and stored with the response. *Acceptance:* a Worker test publishes a form whose total depends on quantity and a conditional discount and asserts the stored total for ten answer sets; publish refuses an expression referencing a deleted question. *Status: missing.*
- **LOGIC-004** — **Quizzes and outcomes.** A flow may score answers (correct answers, points per choice or personality outcome mapping), show the respondent their score, per-question feedback and an outcome screen chosen by score range or top outcome, and report score distributions, per-question correctness and pass rates. *Acceptance:* a Worker test publishes a graded quiz and a personality quiz and asserts scores, chosen outcome screens and the score report for fixed answer sets. *Status: missing.*
- **LOGIC-005** — **Conditional completion and redirect.** A flow may choose its completion screen, or redirect to an HTTPS URL on an approved origin with declared values appended, based on answers, calculations or scores. *Acceptance:* a browser journey takes two paths and lands on two different completion targets; a redirect to an unapproved origin is refused at publish. *Status: missing.*
- **LANG-001** — **Multi-language forms.** A form may have several languages (English and Spanish first-class for US buyers, any BCP 47 language allowed); the respondent sees the language matching their browser with a visible switcher, all steps, choices, errors, disclosures and emails are translated, and stable IDs are shared across languages so results merge with language as a filter. Missing translations block publish for that language. Operators export and import translations as CSV or XLIFF, and may request an AI draft translation that is applied only after review (AI-002). *Acceptance:* a browser journey answers the same form in English and Spanish; results show one distribution with a language filter; publish is refused while one Spanish choice is untranslated. *Status: missing.*
- **AI-002** — **Reviewed AI translation.** An operator can ask `ai.provider.v1` to draft a translation of every untranslated string; the draft is shown beside the source and applies only to strings the operator accepts. *Acceptance:* a Worker test with the AI receiver fixture drafts Spanish strings, accepts half, and the rest stay untranslated and block publish for Spanish. *Status: missing.*
- **A11Y-001** — **WCAG 2.2 AA respondent experience.** Every respondent page, layout, question type, error, progress indicator and completion screen meets WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard operation, visible focus, labels and required semantics, error association, 4.5:1 text contrast with the saved brand, reflow at 320 CSS pixels, reduced motion and screen-reader announcements on step change. *Acceptance:* browser journeys run axe and keyboard-only completion over every question type at phone and desktop widths with zero violations. *Status: partial — axe and keyboard checks cover current types and the operator shell (`tests/e2e/question-accessibility-foundation.spec.ts`, `tests/e2e/accessibility-foundation.spec.ts`); new types and layouts above are not covered.*
- **A11Y-002** — **Accessibility checker before publish.** The builder flags missing alt text, low-contrast brand choices, unlabeled matrix rows, overly long choice lists, timed elements and ambiguous link text, and blocks publish for failures that would breach A11Y-001 with a fix link to each step. *Acceptance:* a Worker test publishes a draft with an image lacking alt text and a 2:1 contrast accent and receives both stable step IDs and reasons. *Status: partial — accent contrast is enforced and logos are validated (`src/core/features/brand/brand.ts`, `tests/runtime/brand.worker.test.ts`); there is no per-step checker.*

## Audience

- **AUD-001 — Contacts.** Operators can create, edit, import, and delete contacts with name, email, and custom fields. Email addresses compare case-insensitively and an import reports, rather than silently duplicating, matching contacts.
- **AUD-002 — Segments.** A segment filters contacts by stored fields. Launching invitations records the participant set plus evaluated `flow.audience-eligibility.v1` and `flow.reminder-schedule.v1` configuration, so later contact, segment, or policy changes do not rewrite the launch; AUD-006 still enforces current hard consent and existence checks at send time.
- **AUD-003 — Participants.** Each invited contact becomes one participant with an opaque invitation token, independent queued/sent/bounced/failed delivery status, eligible/opted-out consent status, and not-started/started/completed response status. Delivery attempts are append-only, and names, emails, and other contact values never appear in the public URL.
- **AUD-004 — Eligibility.** Invitations are created only for contacts allowed by the audience policy; that policy may narrow but never admit a missing-email or opted-out contact. *Policy: `flow.audience-eligibility.v1`; default: also exclude contacts already invited to that published revision.*
- **AUD-005 — Launch idempotency.** Repeating the same audience-launch command for one revision returns the original launch and cannot create duplicate participants, invitation jobs, or delivery attempts.
- **AUD-006 — Send-time eligibility.** Immediately before each invitation or reminder, Pencil verifies that the contact still exists and has not opted out. Mail uses the participant email snapshot displayed and recorded at launch; editing the contact later never silently reroutes an active participant.

## Distribution

- **DIST-001 — Public link.** Every open revision has a canonical direct link that renders the respondent experience without an operator session.
- **DIST-002 — Embed.** One asynchronous script or HTTPS iframe embeds the same respondent experience. It does not block the host page, and direct and embedded submissions produce the same response data and validation. Script embeds use an asynchronously loaded installation-origin script that mounts a sandboxed iframe without allow-same-origin, resizes to its content and emits only loaded, height and completed events. Exact allowed host origin, exact frame source, opaque child origin, challenge and field shape are checked. No answers, response identifiers, credentials or contact details enter the host URL, DOM or messages. The plain iframe option remains available.
- **DIST-003 — Invitations.** An operator can send an invitation to eligible participants. Each message names the business and flow, contains that participant's opaque link, and records a durable delivery attempt before calling `mail.sender.v1`.
- **DIST-004 — Reminders.** Reminders are sent only to sent participants who have not completed, opted out, bounced, or reached a closed or archived flow. Completion cancels pending reminders. *Policy: `flow.reminder-schedule.v1`; default: one reminder 2 days after the invitation, released within the WS-002 sending hours.*
- **DIST-005 — Opt-out.** Every invitation and reminder includes an opt-out link. Opting out prevents future flow mail to that contact regardless of policy. Renewed consent records its actor, time, and source before mail can resume; opt-out does not delete an existing response.
- **DIST-006 — Mail retry.** A transient `mail.sender.v1` failure is retried at most 5 times over 1 hour using the original delivery idempotency key.
- **DIST-007 — Adapter isolation.** Mail cannot be unconfigured (OPER-003). A failing `mail.sender.v1` disables invitations, reminders, notification mail, and new magic-link sign-ins with a named error visible to operators, while existing operator sessions, direct links, embeds, response collection, results, the ledger, and exports keep working. Recovery is restoring the sender; Pencil offers no password or bypass sign-in.
- **DIST-008 — Terminal delivery.** A permanent mail failure or exhausted retry is marked failed with the provider reason and retry action visible to operators; no invitation is recorded as sent merely because it was queued.
- **DIST-009 — Embed isolation.** The embed rejects a host origin not allowed by BASE-PUBLIC-002 before rendering the flow. It exposes no answer, response ID, participant token, or contact value to the host URL, DOM, or `postMessage`; host communication is limited to non-identifying loaded, height, and completed events.
- **DIST-010 — Ambiguous mail outcome.** `mail.sender.v1` implements send-or-get by delivery idempotency key when its provider supports lookup. An ambiguous timeout without lookup is marked unknown and is not automatically retried; an operator may reconcile or explicitly resend it with the duplicate-delivery risk shown.

## Responding

- **RESP-001 — One question at a time.** A respondent sees one welcome, question, or completion screen at a time, with no builder or operator controls.
- **RESP-002 — Truthful progress.** The page shows the respondent's current position and remaining reachable questions for the answers already given. Branching can change that count, and skipped or unreachable questions are never counted.
- **RESP-003 — Answers.** The respondent can submit only the values FLOW-019 defines for the published question's type and configured limits. Every question begins unanswered. False yes/no answers and zero scores or numbers count only after explicit selection or entry. Rating, priority, and choice questions begin unselected and require affirmative respondent selection. Required fields show Required and expose required semantics; a required unanswered or invalid question stays on screen with a field-specific error and creates no response. Progress is the share of currently reachable questions answered validly or deliberately skipped when optional; remaining includes every unfinished reachable question. Skipping never creates an answer.
- **RESP-004 — Back navigation.** A respondent can move back through screens already visited in the current session and edit an answer. Pencil recomputes the forward path and progress and discards answers belonging to steps that become unreachable before final validation, export, or analysis.
- **RESP-005 — Privacy disclosure.** Before the first answer, the flow states why feedback is requested, whether the response is anonymous or identified, who can see individual answers, and each configured external and data category declared in `seed.json → externals` that may receive the answers. The policy decides which identity mode applies; it can never suppress or weaken the disclosure of the mode it chose. *Policy: `response.identity.v1`; default: public-link responses are anonymous and invited responses are identified.*
- **RESP-006 — Anonymous means anonymous.** An anonymous response records no contact link, no participant, no name or email taken from an invitation, contact record, or session, no raw IP address, and no host-page identifier. It receives a random response ID that cannot be reversed to identify the respondent; expiring rate-limit and session state is not linked to that ID. An email or name the respondent knowingly types into a question is stored as that answer and nothing else: it creates no contact and no participant, and RESP-005's disclosure states before the flow starts that answering such a question identifies the respondent to the operator.
- **RESP-007 — Final submission.** A completed response, all its answers, count change, and durable outbox commands for required downstream effects are committed in one transaction before any effect is dispatched.
- **RESP-008 — Admission per invitation.** An invitation admits only the completions its recorded admission rule allows. A visit beyond that shows the existing completion state and creates no further response, and no rule can admit a completion after the flow's admission has closed (RESP-009). *Policy: `flow.response-admission.v1`; default: one completion per invitation; anonymous direct links allow multiple independent completions.*
- **RESP-009 — Concurrent closure.** Final submission rechecks deadline, target, and manual closure in the same admission transaction. A submission that loses the final available place sees the standard closed page naming the business and flow and creates no response.
- **RESP-010 — No silent partials.** Answers from an abandoned or reloaded session are not visible to operators, included in results, exported as responses, or counted as completions. Only final submission creates response data in the deployment.
- **RESP-011 — Completion.** After a successful submission the respondent sees the published completion screen and cannot edit the completed response through the public link.
- **RESP-012 — Submission retry.** A submission idempotency key is scoped to one respondent session and published revision and retained with the completed response. Retrying that key returns the same response ID and completion screen and cannot increment counts or dispatch downstream effects twice.
- **RESP-013 — Session metrics.** The first respondent interaction creates an expiring session record containing only an opaque session ID, revision ID, source, start time, the stable ID of the furthest step reached, and a device class of mobile, tablet, or desktop—never answers, contact identity, raw IP, user-agent string, or host identity. It pins the revision and supplies starts, drop-off, device, and completion-time metrics; an abandoned session expires under BASE-DATA-002 after the retention period in `seed.json → limits`.
- **RESP-014 — Submit failure.** A final-submit request that is not durably committed leaves the respondent off the completion screen, preserves current in-browser answers, shows a retryable error, and retries with the original submission idempotency key.
- **RESP-015 — Abuse defences.** Every public submission is checked by defences the deployment owns entirely: a hidden honeypot field, a minimum elapsed time from session start to final submission taken from `seed.json → limits`, a submission token bound to the RESP-013 session, and the public rate limits enforced under BASE-ACCESS-003. Pencil loads no third-party challenge, script, or reputation service on a public surface, so BASE-PUBLIC-001 continues to hold. A submission whose session token is missing or unrecognised is refused under RESP-014 with a visible, retryable error naming the expired session, never silently discarded. Scoring a respondent's trustworthiness beyond these checks is out of scope.
- **RESP-016 — Bot submissions are dropped.** A submission that fills the honeypot is shown the ordinary completion screen and creates no response, answer, count change, session-metric change, or downstream effect. The drop records an audit entry under BASE-OPS-005 naming the defence that fired, the revision, and the time, and containing no answer text or respondent identity. The honeypot is the only check whose failure discards a submission.
- **RESP-017 — Suspected submissions are quarantined, not lost.** A submission that fails only the minimum-elapsed-time check is committed under RESP-007 as a quarantined response. It is stored complete with its answers, is excluded from completed-response counts, admission checks, results, analysis evidence sets, notifications, and response exports, and is never destroyed automatically.
- **RESP-018 — Quarantine review.** Operators can list quarantined responses for a revision with their answers and the defence that quarantined them, and can release or delete each one. Releasing makes the response an ordinary completed response from that moment, and every affected count, rate, and results figure is recomputed; deleting follows DATA-002. A release is allowed after the revision's admission has closed, is recorded with its operator and time, and never reopens admission or re-triggers a deadline or target check. Nothing leaves quarantine without an operator acting.
- **RESP-019 — Retained flow metrics.** When a session completes or expires, its start, furthest-reached step ID, device class, and completion outcome are folded into per-revision counters that hold counts only, cannot be traced to a session, response, or respondent, and are retained with the revision after the session record has expired. These counters are the source of every historical starts, drop-off, and device figure; no partial answer is retained with them.

- **RESP-020** — **Respondent confirmation email.** A form may send the respondent a branded confirmation (BRAND-001) after completion, optionally with a copy of their answers, their quiz result (LOGIC-004), their payment receipt link (PAY-001) or the signed PDF (SIGN-001), using recall (LOGIC-001). It goes only to an email the respondent typed into a question marked "send my confirmation here" or to an identified participant, is delivered through `mail.sender.v1` with DIST-006 retries, and never contains answers the operator marked sensitive (GOV-004). *Acceptance:* a Worker test completes a form with a confirmation question and asserts one delivery with the escaped answer copy, none for an anonymous response without the question, and a retry on transient failure. *Status: missing — the on-screen completion screen ships under RESP-011.*
- **RESP-021** — **Edit after submission.** A form may allow a respondent to edit their submission until a deadline through a single-use edit link sent with the confirmation; an edit creates a new version of the response with the prior version retained, recomputes results and notifies integrations with an `updated` event. *Acceptance:* a Worker test edits a submission through its link, sees both versions in the ledger and an `updated` webhook, and a second use of the same link is refused. *Status: missing.*
- **PART-001** — **Consented partial-response capture.** A form may opt in to keeping partial responses. The disclosure (RESP-005) states before the first answer that answers are saved as you go; each completed step is then stored against the RESP-013 session, shown to operators in a separate Partial view with the last step reached, excluded from completed counts, admission checks, analysis evidence and default exports unless an operator includes them, and deleted after a retention period (default 30 days) unless completed. Without the opt-in RESP-010 holds unchanged; on activation RESP-010 and the abandoned-session non-goal are amended to name this exception. *Acceptance:* a Worker test answers three of six steps on an opted-in form and finds one partial with three answers that is absent from counts and analysis; the same journey on a form without the opt-in stores nothing; a fake clock past retention deletes the partial. *Status: missing.*
- **PART-002** — **Save and resume.** A respondent may choose "Save and finish later" and receive a resume link by email; the link resumes the same revision and answers on any device until it expires (default 30 days) or the form closes, the email is used only for that link unless the respondent also answers an email question, and a used or expired link shows where to start again. *Acceptance:* a browser journey saves after two steps, opens the emailed link in a new context, sees both answers, completes, and the link then shows the completed state. *Status: missing.*
- **RESP-022** — **Response-quality flags.** Beyond RESP-015's defences, each completed response is checked for straight-lining on grids, completion far faster than the form's median, duplicate open text across responses and gibberish text. A flagged response is quarantined with the reasons (RESP-017, RESP-018) and nothing is discarded; operators set which checks apply per form. On activation the trust-scoring exclusion in RESP-015 is amended to name these checks. *Acceptance:* a Worker test submits a straight-lined grid and a 3-second completion against a 90-second median, both land in quarantine with their reasons, and release restores them to results. *Status: partial — minimum-time quarantine and operator release ship (`src/core/features/responses/service.ts`, `tests/runtime/journey.worker.test.ts`).*

## Response ledger

- **LEDG-001 — Ledger.** Operators can list and open every released completed response with stable response ID, flow revision, source, participant when identified, started and submitted times, and each answer keyed by stable step ID. A quarantined response appears only in the RESP-018 review list until it is released.
- **LEDG-002 — Counts.** Completed-response and participant counts are derived from stored participants and completed responses. Starts come only from RESP-013 session records and the RESP-019 counters they fold into; preview sessions, duplicate submissions, failed deliveries, abandoned sessions, dropped submissions, and unreleased quarantined responses never increment completed responses.
- **LEDG-003 — Filters.** Operators can filter the ledger by revision, submission time, source, participant status, and structured answer, while preserving the same response IDs shown in exports and evidence.
- **LEDG-004 — Response export.** Starting an export fixes its filter and evidence-cutoff time. CSV and JSON contain exactly the matching released responses committed by that cutoff, including response and revision IDs, source, timestamps, stable step IDs and labels, and lossless values for every answer including open text. Quarantined responses are excluded until released, and the export names how many were excluded.

## Results

- **RSLT-001 — Response summary.** Every published revision has a results view that needs no `ai.provider.v1`: question answer summaries appear before session metrics. It shows completed responses, starts, completion rate, and average time to complete for the current filter, each beside the counts it was computed from. Imported completions remain in answer summaries and completed counts but are excluded from native starts, completion-rate numerators and duration samples, with the imported count stated separately. It is available from publication, shows zeros rather than an error before the first session, and never blocks on an analysis run.
- **RSLT-002 — Drop-off by question.** Results show, for each step on the published revision, how many sessions reached it and how many of those reached no later step, computed from RESP-019 counters and therefore still available after the underlying sessions have expired. A step no session reached is shown as reached by zero, not omitted.
- **RSLT-003 — Stated denominators.** Every percentage Pencil displays in results or analysis names the numerator and denominator it was computed from. A single-answer distribution divides by the responses that answered that question and its parts sum to that denominator. A multiple-choice distribution divides by the same denominator, may therefore total more than 100%, and is labelled as multiple-select. Completion rate divides native completed responses by native starts for the same revision and filter, excluding imported historical responses from both rate and duration calculations, and is never mixed with a per-question answer rate. Changing a filter changes both numerator and denominator together.
- **RSLT-004 — Device split.** Results show the share of sessions in each RESP-013 device class, divided by the sessions counted for that revision and filter and named as RSLT-003 requires. Device class is derived at session start, is never stored on a response, and never appears in an export of responses.
- **RSLT-005 — Results across revisions.** A results view spanning more than one revision reports each question only for the revisions that contained it, using the responses that could have answered it as the denominator. It never treats a question added later as unanswered by earlier responses, and it never merges two questions that have different stable step IDs.

### Target: reports buyers share

- **RSLT-006** — **Crosstabs.** An operator can cross any structured question, hidden field, collector, language, calculated value or score against another, with counts, row and column percentages named under RSLT-003, a chi-square test flagged only when every expected cell count is at least 5, and ANLY-005 suppression for cells below the disclosure threshold. *Acceptance:* a Worker test crosses a 3-choice question with a 2-choice question over a fixed dataset and asserts every cell, percentage, the chi-square statistic and a suppressed small cell. *Status: missing.*
- **RSLT-007** — **Trends over time.** Results chart any score or answer share by day, week, month or quarter in the WS-001 zone, with the per-period denominator, a minimum-period-size rule, and revision boundaries marked; recurring occurrences (DIST-016) and programs (PROG-003) use the same series. *Acceptance:* a Worker test seeds responses across three months and asserts weekly and monthly NPS, denominators and a revision marker; a period below the minimum shows no value. *Status: missing.*
- **RSLT-008** — **Saved views and dashboards.** An operator can save a named view (filters, compared cohorts, crosstabs, chosen charts) and arrange several into a form or program dashboard; views reopen with the same numbers for the same cutoff and record who saved them. *Acceptance:* a browser journey saves a filtered crosstab view, reloads it as another operator, and sees identical figures. *Status: partial — filters and revision comparisons ship (`src/core/features/reporting/answerFilter.ts`, `tests/runtime/ledger-filters.worker.test.ts`); saved views do not.*
- **RSLT-009** — **Shared report links.** A saved view can be shared as a read-only link with optional password and expiry, revocable at any time; it shows aggregates only, applies ANLY-005 suppression, never shows respondent identity, excerpts below the threshold, uploads or payment details, and sets no cookies beyond its own session. *Acceptance:* a browser journey opens a shared link without an operator session, sees the aggregates, finds no response ID or identity in the page, and gets 404 after revocation. *Status: missing.*
- **RSLT-010** — **Report exports.** Any view exports to PDF, PowerPoint (PPTX) and XLSX with its charts, denominators and filters stated on each page. *Acceptance:* a Worker test exports a view to each format and asserts each contains the stated filter text and the same counts as the view. *Status: missing.*
- **RSLT-011** — **Scheduled report email.** An operator can schedule a view to be emailed weekly or monthly to named operators as aggregates with a link to the authenticated view, under NOTIFY-004's no-reply rules and WS-002 hours. *Acceptance:* a Worker test advances a fake clock to two scheduled sends and asserts two deliveries with aggregates and no answer text or respondent identity. *Status: missing.*

## Analysis

- **ANLY-001 — Analysis run.** An operator can request analysis for any revision with completed responses. A run is queued durably and shows queued, running, completed, insufficient-evidence, or failed status. *Policy: `analysis.autorun.v1`; default: run when a flow closes and has at least 5 completed responses.*
- **ANLY-002 — Fixed evidence set.** An analysis records the exact response IDs and revision it read, and reads only released responses. Responses submitted, or released from quarantine, after it started are excluded and make the completed analysis stale rather than changing it in place.
- **ANLY-003 — Findings.** A run that reaches completed status ranks themes and selects one primary finding from its fixed evidence set; an insufficient-evidence run follows ANLY-013 instead.
- **ANLY-004 — Exact evidence.** Text presented as a respondent quote is an exact excerpt of a stored answer. Generated paraphrases and summaries are labelled as analysis and never presented in quotation marks or attributed to a respondent.
- **ANLY-005 — Disclosure threshold.** In every analysis view, a theme or filtered segment with fewer included responses than the threshold shows no raw excerpt, participant identity, unique field combination, or breakdown; exactly the threshold is permitted. Suppression changes presentation, not the raw ledger or operator export. *Policy: `analysis.disclosure.v1`; default threshold: 5 completed responses in that theme or current filter cohort.*
- **ANLY-006 — Recommendations.** A completed analysis with sufficient evidence may propose one primary action with rationale, linked findings, confidence, expected impact, and effort; an insufficient-evidence run proposes none. *Policy: `recommendation.selection.v1`; default: the action supported by the highest-impact finding with sufficient evidence.*
- **ANLY-007 — Staleness.** A stale analysis remains readable and labelled with its evidence cutoff. Operators can run a new analysis; prior runs remain in history except for the erasure redaction required by DATA-002.
- **ANLY-008 — Provider failure.** If `ai.provider.v1` is unconfigured or fails an analysis or builder-assistance request, the request is marked failed with the error and retry action, no suggestion, finding, or recommendation is fabricated, and flow publishing, response collection, the ledger, and exports continue to work.
- **ANLY-009 — Builder assistance.** An operator may request a sharper, broader, or contextual rewrite of a draft question. Suggestions show their source text and are never applied or published without an operator accepting them.
- **ANLY-010 — Cost.** Every AI call records provider, model, input and output usage, feature, analysis or draft ID, and reported cost. Operators can view totals by day and feature.
- **ANLY-011 — Evidence provenance.** Each displayed theme count, percentage, supporting excerpt, and recommendation records the included response IDs from which it was derived; an operator can navigate from displayed evidence to those retained responses.
- **ANLY-012 — Output validation.** Before a run completes, Pencil validates its schema, verifies that cited response IDs belong to the run's evidence set, verifies quote ranges exactly against stored answers, and recomputes counts and percentages over that evidence set; any failure marks the run failed.
- **ANLY-013 — Insufficient evidence.** A run whose evidence or disclosure-eligible cohorts cannot support a finding completes as `insufficient-evidence`, shows the evidence cutoff and reason, and emits no theme, quote, or recommendation.
- **ANLY-014 — Untrusted evidence.** Respondent text is treated only as data: it cannot change system instructions or configuration, select tools, call adapters, create jobs, or authorize effects; schema-invalid provider output fails under ANLY-012.
- **ANLY-015 — Analysis policy snapshot.** Each run stores the evaluated `analysis.disclosure.v1` and `recommendation.selection.v1` configuration; later policy changes affect only a new run and never retroactively reveal or reinterpret historical output.
- **ANLY-016 — Provider retry.** A transient `ai.provider.v1` transport failure is retried at most 3 times over 10 minutes with the same request, evidence set, and policy snapshot; schema or evidence-validation failures are terminal and never retried as transport failures.

## Actions and measurement

- **ACT-001 — Action record.** An operator can accept a recommendation as an action with title, rationale, owner, target metric, review date, and links to its analysis and evidence. Accepting does not itself contact an external service.
- **ACT-002 — Action lifecycle.** A new action is planned. Legal transitions are planned → in progress or closed, in progress → ready for review or closed, ready for review → effective, ineffective, in progress, or closed, and effective or ineffective → closed; every transition records the operator, time, and optional outcome note.
- **ACT-003 — External task.** `task.destination.v1` must implement create-or-get by Pencil's idempotency key and return the existing remote task ID for a repeated key; repeated activation or delivery of one action therefore cannot create more than one external task.
- **ACT-004 — Task failure.** An unconfigured adapter disables external task creation with setup guidance. A failed call keeps the local action, records the attempt and error, and offers a retry that reuses the same idempotency key.
- **ACT-005 — Follow-up measurement.** An action records a metric name, unit, desired direction, baseline value, source, and observation time. An operator can link a later flow or analysis, record the comparable observed value and time, and mark the outcome effective or ineffective. Pencil never sets or changes that verdict itself, and no extension point can; a review date that has passed is surfaced on the action, never resolved on the operator's behalf.
- **ACT-006 — Confirmed task payload.** Before calling `task.destination.v1`, Pencil shows the action fields and operator-authenticated report link that will leave the deployment and sends only those confirmed values; raw answers and excerpts are never included.
- **ACT-007 — Task delivery state.** External task delivery records pending, confirmed, unknown, or failed status, the idempotency key, every attempt, and the remote task ID when confirmed. An unknown network outcome is retried with the same key rather than assumed failed or sent again with a new key.
- **ACT-008 — Manual recommendation.** An operator may write a recommendation by hand and accept it as an action under ACT-001, linked to the results evidence it rests on: a flow, a published revision and optionally one question, with the RSLT counts shown at that moment kept as they were, never response identifiers, answer excerpts or cohort filters. An action written by hand has no analysis link. The analysis link of ACT-001 is required for every action accepted from an analysis once ANLY is implemented; nothing else in ACT-001 to ACT-007 is relaxed.

### Target: actions that are chased

- **ACT-009** — **Action owner reminders.** An action's owner is reminded by email and, when connected, Slack or Teams (INTEG-006) before its review date, on the date and weekly after it while unresolved, within WS-002 hours; a linked external task's status (INTEG-013) is shown on the action and refreshed. Pencil still never sets the verdict (ACT-005). *Acceptance:* a Worker test advances a fake clock across the review date and asserts the before, on-date and overdue reminders, then a verdict by the owner stops them. *Status: partial — review dates are recorded and surfaced when passed (`src/core/features/actions/service.ts`, `tests/runtime/actions-measurement.worker.test.ts`); reminders are not sent.*

## Notifications

- **NOTIFY-001 — Analysis attention.** The in-app notification centre records one notification for the requesting operator when an analysis completes or fails; an automatic close-time analysis records one for every unmuted operator. Duplicate queue delivery cannot create the same notification twice.
- **NOTIFY-002 — New-response mail.** Each operator sets, per flow, whether completed responses are mailed to them immediately, grouped daily under NOTIFY-003, or not mailed. Publication starts its publisher on Immediately only when no preference exists; every other operator defaults Off. Existing unrepublished flows default Off. A message uses the saved business identity under BRAND-001, names the flow and submission time in the workspace zone, and links to the authenticated results ledger; it never carries answer text, respondent identity, or a link that grants access without signing in. A committed, released response queues one delivery per opted-in operator, with durable attempts, bounded retries and explicit unknown-outcome confirmation. Daily uses NOTIFY-003 and keeps its recipient snapshot separate from immediate mail.
- **NOTIFY-003 — Digest.** A digest is one message per operator per flow per day, sent at the WS-002 window's opening hour in the WS-001 zone, covering the completed responses committed since the previous digest. A day with no new completed responses sends no message, and a digest that fails to send is retried under DIST-006 rather than merging into the next day's.
- **NOTIFY-004 — No mail loops.** Every notification, digest, invitation, and reminder is sent from the configured no-reply address (RER's assigned verified sender on Managed hosting) and marked auto-generated. Pencil ingests no inbound mail, so a reply or vacation auto-responder can never create a response, participant, notification, or further outbound mail. A bounce of operator notification or digest mail is recorded against that operator's mail setting only and never marks a participant bounced; participant bounce status comes only from invitation and reminder delivery (DIST-008).

### Target: alerts

## Form imports

- **IMPORT-001 — Reviewed definition.** Operators can import a documented form-definition JSON into a new editable draft with new stable IDs. Every field, screen and logic action is listed before writes with source type, imported/changed/unsupported outcome, explicit change and reason. Unsupported logic leaves affected paths incomplete; native graph validation still controls publication.
- **IMPORT-002 — Reviewed CSV.** Operators can map every response CSV column to a supported question, timestamp or Skip. Every row error is reported. Blank answers stay unanswered; malformed values and configured capacity excess are refused before commit. Commit requires acknowledgement of every reported change or omission; cancelling review creates no job, flow or response. Acknowledgment first stores owner-private, indexed preparation with bounded database transport. - **IMPORT-003 — Historical privacy and pinning.** Imported released completions retain their original timestamps and remain anonymous, with source imported and an immutable revision matching the mapped source. - **IMPORT-004 — Durable replay and reversal.** The unchanged Base D1 import kit owns reviewed commit, source identity, bounded resume and undo. Identical files create no duplicate native records; interruption resumes the exact stored safe plan. Undo removes unedited imported data while exact native snapshot guards preserve and report later edits. Completed reversal is idempotent; retained safe plan and provenance survive. ## Push further

Target. Agents serve the whole job beyond what Typeform, SurveyMonkey, Tally, Fillout and Formbricks offer today. Every agent reads only data the requesting operator may see, runs through `ai.provider.v1` with ANLY-012 to ANLY-016's validation, cost recording and untrusted-evidence rules, cites stable response IDs and exact excerpts, never invents an answer or a respondent, and takes no outward effect (publishing, sending, connecting, deleting or contacting anyone) without an operator approving that specific effect.

- **AGENT-001** — **Study designer.** From a business question ("why are trial users not converting?") and optional context (prior forms, findings, CRM segments), the agent drafts a short study: the decision it informs, hypotheses, questions with neutral wording, a branching plan, the audience and channel, the sample needed for a stated margin of error, and the analysis plan. - **AGENT-002** — **Themes and contradictions.** Over a fixed evidence set (ANLY-002), the agent codes open text into themes with exact excerpts and also finds contradictions: cohorts whose open text disagrees with their scores, themes that reverse between revisions, collectors or segments, and questions whose answers conflict within one response. ## Honest boundaries

What Pencil does not do, and how a US buyer gets it.

- **Holding money or card data.** Payments are processed by Stripe, PayPal or Square on their hosted pages (PAY-001 to PAY-005); Pencil never stores card or bank numbers and is not a payment facilitator. Ongoing subscription billing, dunning and invoicing belong to Stripe Billing or the sibling Nectar; storefronts to Stall.
- **Survey panels and representative samples.** Pencil surveys the business's own customers, employees and visitors. Buyers who need recruited respondents or population-representative samples buy them from SurveyMonkey Audience, Cint, Prolific or Pollfish and send those respondents to a Pencil collector; results are never presented as representative of a population.
- **Cross-customer benchmarks.** Each installation is one business's data, so Pencil offers no industry NPS benchmarks drawn from other customers; operators can enter published benchmarks as reference lines.
- **Multi-party e-signature and identity verification.** Signature fields (SIGN-001) cover single-signer consent forms; envelopes, signing order, notarization, KBA or ID verification belong to the sibling Inkwell or Docusign.
- **Regulated decisions.** Pencil collects and analyses answers; credit, insurance, hiring, medical or identity decisions are made by the buyer's qualified provider and workflow, never by Pencil's analysis or agents.
- **HIPAA on its own.** HIPAA mode (GOV-001) enforces Pencil's side, but the owner must hold BAAs with Cloudflare (an Enterprise agreement), the mail provider and any connected service; Pencil as owned software signs no BAA itself.
- **Third-party challenges, pixels and trackers.** Respondent pages load nothing from third-party origins (BASE-PUBLIC-001, RESP-015): no CAPTCHA service, ad pixel or analytics tag. Conversion tracking is done server-side through webhooks (INTEG-010) into the buyer's analytics or the sibling Vane.
- **Appointment availability, CRM, email marketing, help desk, feedback boards, general automation and databases.** Dial, Orbit, Mango, HappyToHelp, Penny, Crank and Cradle (or the named incumbents) own these; Pencil's integrations hand over to them.
- **De-anonymizing feedback.** Anonymous responses stay anonymous (RESP-006); no integration, prefill or agent links them to a person.
- **SMS carrier registration.** A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration is done in the owner's Twilio account; Pencil shows its state but cannot register on the owner's behalf.
- **Children's data.** Forms are not directed at children under 13; a buyer who needs COPPA-compliant collection uses a provider that offers verifiable parental consent.

## Managed installation

Signed installation admission at `/auth/owner-claim` uses the maintained Base
owner-claim and operator-auth modules. Trusted deployment configuration supplies
its issuer, installation identity, exact origin, owner mailbox and public keys.
A confirmation GET writes nothing; signed POST creates native authority once.
Any existing claim or operator permanently refuses replacement.

The optional editor at `/app` uses an active native owner session and short-lived
same-origin `/api/editor/config` grants. No secret is returned. Native signout
revokes the mount. Documents allow only the configured management origin for
embedding and preserve their other CSP protections. The published visual edits
are data, compiled into the owned source and applied by one local runtime.
